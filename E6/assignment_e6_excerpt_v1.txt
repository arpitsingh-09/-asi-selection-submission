Budget. Choose M dividing K (for example K = 6, M = 3). Every arm returns exactly
K final hypotheses under the same cap on generated tokens per input set. Report the
tokens actually used.
Example (One input set, two arms)
Inputs: the references of a 2026 paper on contrastive pre-training. D writes six hypotheses
directly, e.g. “Stronger crops improve linear-probe accuracy on small datasets.” QH
first writes three questions, e.g. “How does augmentation strength interact with batch
size in contrastive pre-training?” and then two hypotheses per question, e.g. “Optimal
augmentation strength decreases as batch size grows.” Both arms are then scored by the
same evaluation.
Two testbeds.
1. Literature. At least 100 input sets, each the references (at least 10 with abstracts)
of a paper posted after your generator’s training cutoff. The target is that paper’s
own research question and main hypothesis; extract them with one fixed prompt
and check 20 by hand.
2. Data. Tasks from [R44], where a hypothesis is scored by how well it explains held-out
data and, on synthetic tasks, by whether it recovers the planted hypothesis.
Predict first - Which arm wins on each testbed and on each metric of Task 2 and by how
much? Will selection beat random choice?
